# Controversies Research

Documented F1 stewarding controversies, 2023–2025, gathered as candidate
targets for the scraper and eval set. Terms ("Divergent Precedent",
"Superseded Precedent") used exactly per `CONTEXT.md`. Article numbers are
best-guess from reporting, not verified against the regulation text -- that
verification happens against the article change history (real Issue diffs).

---

## 1. Austin 2023 — Mass track limits penalty saga (COTA)

- **Event / date:** United States Grand Prix, Austin, 22 October 2023.
- **Cars / drivers:** Widespread — 35 lap times deleted; several drivers
  penalised after the race, including a penalty for Lewis Hamilton (car 44)
  that moved him behind teammate George Russell.
- **Likely cited Article:** 33.3 (track limits / leaving the track).
- **Why contested:** Coming right after 51 deletions at Qatar and a season
  of 84 deletions at the Austrian GP, stewards themselves called their
  ability to enforce a single standard "completely unsatisfactory" — a
  self-admission of inconsistency, not just fan complaint.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent (same Article, same season, wildly
  different enforcement outcomes race to race and driver to driver).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.racefans.net/2023/11/09/stewards-again-urge-fix-for-completely-unsatisfactory-enforcement-of-track-limits/
  - https://www.motorsinside.com/en/f1/news/35027-the-lack-of-consistency-in-decisions-of-fia-once-again-under-scrutiny-in-austin
  - https://racingnews365.com/key-element-to-controversial-austin-f1-steward-decision-raised

## 2. Austrian GP 2023 — Red Bull Ring Turn 10, 1200+ violations

- **Event / date:** Austrian Grand Prix, Spielberg, 2 July 2023 (race
  weekend); 12 post-race penalties issued, 8 drivers affected.
- **Cars / drivers:** 7 drivers penalised in-race; 12 further penalties
  post-race including Lewis Hamilton (car 44), moved from a 5s to a 10s
  penalty, dropping him behind Russell to 8th.
- **Likely cited Article:** 33.3.
- **Why contested:** Turn 10's kerb/track-edge geometry produced over
  1,200 logged violations across the weekend; the difference between a
  flagged and unflagged pass came down to a binary all-four-wheels sensor
  reading that looked arbitrary on broadcast, prompting fans and teams to
  question why visually similar off-track moments were treated differently.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent (same corner, same Article, inconsistent
  application within a single event) — also a **Superseded Precedent**
  seed, since this event is repeatedly cited as the trigger for the 2024
  semi-automated detection system and later Driving Standards Guidelines
  revisions.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/fia-hands-out-12-penalties-for-track-limit-offences-after-f1-austrian-gp/10490842/
  - https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/1086732/f1-austrian-grand-prix-track-limits-problem-explained/

## 3. Singapore GP 2023 — Verstappen impeding, stewards admit error

- **Event / date:** Singapore Grand Prix qualifying, 16 September 2023;
  admission of error reported ~22 September 2023 (ahead of Japanese GP).
- **Cars / drivers:** Max Verstappen (car 1) impeding Yuki Tsunoda
  (AlphaTauri) and Logan Sargeant (Williams) in separate Q1/Q2 incidents.
- **Likely cited Article:** 39.4 / impeding under qualifying procedure
  regulations (exact sub-article TBD in parsing).
- **Why contested:** Verstappen received only two reprimands and no grid
  penalty, despite "unmistakable" impeding that would normally cost three
  grid places. FIA stewards explicitly stated afterward that the decision
  "will not serve as a precedent" — a direct FIA acknowledgment that this
  outcome diverges from the standard applied to comparable cases.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent (FIA's own words: same offence category,
  different — and self-admitted incorrect — outcome).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.espn.com/f1/story/_/id/38461162/f1-stewards-accept-erred-not-penalising-max-verstappen-singapore-gp
  - https://www.grandprix247.com/2023/09/22/no-verstappen-penalty-in-singapore-an-error-admit-f1-stewards/
  - https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/1004413/f1-stewards-singapore-mistake-max-verstappen-evade-penalty/

## 4. United States GP 2024 — Norris penalised, Verstappen not, same battle

- **Event / date:** United States Grand Prix, Austin, 20 October 2024,
  Lap 52, Turn 12.
- **Cars / drivers:** Lando Norris (car 4) given a 5-second penalty for
  "leaving the track and gaining an advantage" passing Max Verstappen
  (car 1) off-track; Verstappen, who also ran off track defending, was
  not penalised.
- **Likely cited Article:** 33.3, applied alongside the (non-public)
  FIA Driving Standards Guidelines on defending/apex position.
- **Why contested:** Both cars left the track in the same corner of the
  same overtaking move, yet only one was penalised. McLaren's Andrea
  Stella called the penalty "inappropriate"; Zak Brown and Toto Wolff
  separately criticised the stewards as "inconsistent" and "biased" for
  penalising Norris (and Russell) while Verstappen escaped for what they
  called "the same transgressions."
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent (compare directly against Entry 5 —
  visually similar defending-move-off-track scenario, opposite penalty
  weight one week apart).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.espn.com/racing/f1/story/_/id/41916208/lando-norris-max-verstappen-us-gp-austin-cota-duel-brings-question-f1-stewarding-rules
  - https://www.malaymail.com/news/sports/2024/10/21/mclaren-slams-controversial-us-gppenalty-as-inappropriate-as-both-norris-verstappen-went-off-track/154292
  - https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/why-norris-was-penalised-and-what-f1s-driver-guidelines-really-say/10665268/

## 5. Mexico City GP 2024 — Verstappen given 10s (twice) for same category of move Norris got 5s for at Austin

- **Event / date:** Mexico City Grand Prix, 27 October 2024, one week
  after Entry 4.
- **Cars / drivers:** Max Verstappen (car 1) given two separate 10-second
  penalties for forcing Norris off track/defending off-track against
  Norris (car 4).
- **Likely cited Article:** 33.3 plus Driving Standards Guidelines
  provisions on defending position.
- **Why contested:** The penalty weight (10s x2) for what press described
  as a comparable defending-off-track category of move just one race after
  Austin's 5s Norris penalty (Entry 4) raised direct "why different
  weighting for a similar offence" questions — explicitly framed by
  Motorsport.com as needing explanation of the gap.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent — directly paired with Entry 4 as a
  same-category, different-magnitude outcome comparison across
  consecutive events.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/why-verstappen-got-10s-penalties-in-mexico-when-norris-got-5s-at-austin/10668051/

## 6. Qatar GP 2023 Sprint — Leclerc/Stroll penalised, Russell's 4th infringement missed

- **Event / date:** Qatar Grand Prix sprint weekend, Lusail, 6–8 October
  2023.
- **Cars / drivers:** Charles Leclerc (car 16) and Lance Stroll (car 18)
  given 5-second penalties for repeated track limits violations; George
  Russell (car 63) had three lap times deleted and received a
  black-and-white warning flag, but reporting indicates a fourth
  violation (Lap 46, Turn 10 exit) that should have triggered an automatic
  5-second penalty went unactioned by stewards.
- **Likely cited Article:** 33.3, in combination with the automatic
  penalty threshold in the event-specific track limits notes (common at
  Lusail's new-surface circuit).
- **Why contested:** The new automated deletion system was reported as
  "a flop" at this event — the same infringement type triggered automatic
  penalties for some drivers but was reportedly missed for Russell despite
  onboard footage showing a clear fourth violation.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent (same automatic-threshold rule, applied
  to some drivers and apparently missed for another in the same session).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.pitdebrief.com/post/how-russell-escaped-a-penalty-for-a-fourth-track-limits-infringement-at-f1-qatar-gp/
  - https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/leclerc-and-stroll-penalised-for-track-limits-in-f1-qatar-sprint/10530125/
  - https://scuderiafans.com/mass-deletion-of-lap-times-at-lusail-new-anti-track-limits-system-is-a-flop-2023-f1-qatar-gp/

## 7. Australian GP 2023 — Standing restart chaos, Haas protest, inconsistent post-restart penalty application

- **Event / date:** Australian Grand Prix, Melbourne, 2 April 2023,
  final laps / final restart.
- **Cars / drivers:** Multi-car incident on the final standing restart —
  Pierre Gasly, Esteban Ocon, Nyck de Vries, Logan Sargeant crashed out;
  Carlos Sainz (car 55) and Fernando Alonso made contact, with Sainz
  handed a post-race 5-second penalty. Haas protested the classification.
- **Likely cited Article:** 42/48 (safety car and race restart procedure
  articles; exact numbering TBD).
- **Why contested:** Stewards took roughly 30 minutes to decide how to
  classify/restart with one lap remaining after several cars had already
  crashed out earlier in the same restart sequence; drivers who crashed
  in the earlier incident lost their classified positions while the
  order was reshuffled, prompting a formal Haas protest over the fairness
  of the restart/classification procedure itself. Stewards subsequently
  called for the standing-restart regulation to be reviewed.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent candidate for the restart-procedure
  category (a formal protest exists specifically because participants
  believed the procedure was applied unevenly to those still running vs.
  those who had crashed) — also relevant context for later restart
  procedure regulation changes (Superseded Precedent angle, Entry 9).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/breaking-haas-protest-results-of-2023-australian-grand-prix.2VIj7H2Oe3V3AAPGESJOdd
  - https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/f1-stewards-want-review-of-standing-restart-procedures-after-australian-gp-near-miss/10452769/
  - https://www.racefans.net/2023/04/06/got-away-with-that-one-f1-drivers-unheard-reactions-to-australian-gp-restart-chaos/

## 8. Tuscan GP-era safety car restart timing complaints (context precedent, pre-2023 baseline)

- **Event / date:** Referenced repeatedly in 2023 reporting as the
  baseline precedent for restart-timing complaints; relevant again in
  2023 discussions (Australian GP aftermath, Entry 7).
- **Cars / drivers:** General grid complaint, over half the field warned
  after a multi-car restart crash in the referenced season.
- **Likely cited Article:** Safety car restart / lights-out procedure
  articles (same family as Entry 7 and Entry 9).
- **Why contested:** Drivers stated restarts were being handled
  differently race to race — specifically that the safety car's lights
  were turned off later in some races, giving the race leader less time
  to build a gap before the restart, i.e., the same procedural rule
  produced materially different pacing outcomes depending on timing calls
  made lap to lap.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent (procedural application varying without
  a stated rule change to justify it).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/top-f1-drivers-think-safety-car-process-set-up-restart-crash/
  - https://www.espn.com/f1/story/_/id/34577262/does-f1-need-safety-car-rule-rethink/

## 9. Article 33.3 / track limits enforcement — Superseded Precedent (2022→2024 tightening + semi-automated detection)

- **Event / date:** Rule interpretation shift beginning under race
  director Niels Wittich (post-Masi, from 2022) with white-line-only
  standard; further procedural change for 2024 season (semi-automated
  camera detection system introduced).
- **Cars / drivers:** N/A — regulatory/process change, not a single
  incident.
- **Likely cited Article:** 33.3 (text: "Drivers must make every
  reasonable effort to use the track at all times and may not leave the
  track without a justifiable reason" — white lines count as part of the
  track, kerbs do not).
- **Why contested:** Any Decision from the 2023 season (especially
  Austria/Qatar/Austin — Entries 1, 2, 6) that applied Article 33.3 under
  the pre-2024 manual/inconsistent enforcement process should be treated
  as potentially superseded once the semi-automated detection system and
  subsequent Driving Standards Guidelines updates (see Entry 10) changed
  *how* the same Article's standard was actually enforced — a process
  change materially affecting outcome consistency even where the Article
  text itself didn't move.
- **Type:** Superseded Precedent.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/articles/single-seaters/f1/what-are-f1-track-limits-rules-penalties-and-what-is-changing/
  - https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/fia_2024_formula_1_sporting_regulations_-_issue_1_-_2023-09-26.pdf
  - https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/fia_2024_formula_1_sporting_regulations_-_issue_2_-_2023-10-25.pdf

## 10. FIA Driving Standards Guidelines revision — Superseded Precedent (2024 Qatar meeting → v4.1, Feb 2025)

- **Event / date:** Guidelines drafted following a driver meeting at the
  2024 Qatar Grand Prix (late 2024); published update dated 20 February
  2025 (version 4.1).
- **Cars / drivers:** N/A — guideline document revision, applies
  retroactively in spirit to all prior Decisions citing defending/apex
  position standards (relevant to Entries 4 and 5 directly).
- **Likely cited Article:** Not a numbered Sporting Regulation Article
  itself — an interpretive document read alongside Article 33.3 and the
  driving-conduct articles (used by stewards as the operative standard
  for "who has the right to the corner").
- **Why contested:** Reporting explicitly ties the 2024→2025 revision to
  flaws exposed in the prior wording — specifically the "ahead at the
  apex" test used to justify the Norris/Verstappen Austin 2024 penalty
  split (Entry 4) — with the-race.com noting the new rules produced at
  least one 2025 penalty (Verstappen, Jeddah) that would likely have been
  decided differently under the pre-revision wording.
- **Type:** Superseded Precedent (a Decision reasoned under the pre-2025
  Guidelines version no longer reflects the standard stewards apply after
  the v4.1 update).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/revealed-new-f1-2025-racing-rules-verstappen-jeddah-penalty/
  - https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/f1_driving_standards_guidelines_version_4.1_feb_20_2025.pdf
  - https://www.grandprix247.com/formula-1-news/fia-publishes-f1-stewards-guidelines-on-driving-standards-and-penalties-to-boost-transparency

## 11. Standing restart / red flag procedure — Superseded Precedent (post-Australia 2023 regulation tweak)

- **Event / date:** Regulation adjustment reported shortly after the
  2023 Australian GP restart chaos (Entry 7), season 2023 into 2024.
- **Cars / drivers:** N/A — procedural regulation change.
- **Likely cited Article:** Safety car / standing restart procedure
  articles (same family referenced in Entries 7–8).
- **Why contested:** The FIA explicitly stated it would streamline the
  safety car restart process following the Australian GP near-miss and
  driver criticism; any Decision or stewards' reasoning that relied on
  the pre-change restart procedure (e.g., contact/incident calls made
  during the Entry 7 restart) sits on a superseded procedural basis once
  the streamlined process took effect.
- **Type:** Superseded Precedent.
- **Sources:**

## 12. Unsafe release penalty weighting — Divergent Precedent candidate (2023–2024 pit lane incidents)

- **Event / date:** Multiple events across 2023–2024, e.g. Italian GP
  2024 qualifying (McLaren/Piastri), Saudi Arabian GP (Tsunoda), Austrian
  GP 2024 Q3 (Mercedes).
- **Cars / drivers:** Oscar Piastri (car 81, McLaren) — team fined
  €10,000 for unsafe release in qualifying; Yuki Tsunoda (car 22,
  AlphaTauri/RB) — 5-second penalty for unsafe release into the path of
  Lando Norris at Saudi Arabia; Mercedes — €5,000 fine for an unsafe
  release from the garage during Q3 at the Austrian GP 2024, no time
  penalty to the driver.
- **Likely cited Article:** 27 / pit lane procedure articles governing
  safe release (exact sub-article TBD in parsing).
- **Why contested:** The same offence category — an unsafe release
  endangering another driver — produced outcomes ranging from a team-only
  fine with no driver penalty (Mercedes, Austria) to a driver time penalty
  (Tsunoda, Saudi Arabia) to a team fine with no time penalty despite a
  qualifying-session release (Piastri, Italy), with no clear public
  rationale distinguishing the severity tiers applied.
- **Type:** Divergent Precedent.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.si.com/onsi/f1/news/mclaren-slammed-with-10-000-fine-for-oscar-piastri-qualifying-error-01j6msz15pqb
  - https://thesportsrush.com/f1-news-what-is-unsafe-release-in-f1-why-are-drivers-penalized-for-it/
  - https://www.gptoday.net/en/news/f1/249727/stewards-to-use-time-penalties-to-punish-unsafe-releases
